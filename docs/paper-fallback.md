# Switching to paper

Paper stays available for every vote. If the site, the internet or a member's phone fails,
the chair can switch to paper on the spot, in the middle of a vote, without losing the
digital ballots already cast. The site does not take paper results: the paper count is done
by hand, with witnesses, and is the official record while the site is in its pilot.

## When to switch

Any of these is enough, and the chair decides:

- The voting site or the room's internet is not working.
- A member cannot vote on a phone, or does not want to.
- Anyone present asks for it. Nobody has to explain why.

## Before the meeting

- Open the vote on the admin site as usual.
- Print the paper kit now, not when it is needed: **Paper ballots and count sheet** on the
  vote's admin page. Print more ballots than there are members (spoiled ones are replaced).
  The ballots carry no number or mark that could tell one from another, so they cannot be
  linked to a person.
- Keep the printed ballots and one count sheet with the slips.

## How to switch, step by step

1. **The chair announces the switch.**
2. **Close the digital vote** on the admin site (**Close the vote**). From that moment nobody
   can cast a digital ballot, and the slips nobody used are deleted. The digital result is
   final and is shown on the results and audit pages like any closed vote.
3. **Collect the unused slips.** Each member who has not voted digitally hands their slip
   back to the chair and takes one paper ballot. A member who already voted digitally keeps
   their receipt and does not vote again. The chair counts the unused slips returned and
   hands out exactly that many paper ballots.
4. **Members mark and fold** their paper ballot and put it in the box.
5. **Count by hand**, in the open, with two witnesses, using the count sheet. Fill in the
   reconciliation lines at the top:
   - slips issued (printed on the sheet from the site),
   - digital ballots counted (from the site, once closed),
   - unused slips handed back,
   - paper ballots handed out (must equal the slips handed back),
   - paper ballots spoiled and replaced,
   - paper ballots counted.
6. **Check that it adds up.** Digital ballots counted plus paper ballots counted should equal
   the slips issued, less any slip nobody returned and nobody used. Say out loud how any gap
   is explained. A gap that cannot be explained is announced, not hidden.
7. **Announce both results:** the digital result from the site and the paper result from the
   count sheet. The chair announces the combined outcome, adding the two tallies by hand.
   Counts are announced; the site does not combine them.
8. **Sign and keep.** The counter and both witnesses sign the count sheet. Keep it together
   with the paper ballots. They are the record.

## What if the whole vote is on paper?

Skip step 2 and run the vote on paper from the start, with the same ballots and count sheet.
There is then no digital result.

## What the site does and does not do here

- It stops accepting digital ballots when the vote is closed, and publishes the digital result,
  receipts and every anonymous digital ballot as usual.
- It does not record paper counts, and it does not add digital and paper together. Keeping
  paper results off the site means nobody who administers the site can change a paper result.
- Paper ballots are counted by people watching each other. That is the same trust model
  paper has always had, and it is why paper remains the fallback.
