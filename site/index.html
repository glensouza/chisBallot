A secret ballot you can check for yourself.

chisBallot is a small, free, open source voting site for the Citrus Hills Intermediate School council board. No sign-in, no account, and no one can tell how you voted. Afterwards, every voter can confirm their own ballot was counted.

How a vote works

The room, the roster and the chair in charge stay exactly as they are. Only where the ballot is marked changes.

1

Check-in

The chair checks you off the roster, the same as today.

2

Draw a slip

You pick any folded slip from the pile. Each has a QR code, so nobody knows which one you hold.

3

Scan and vote

Your ballot opens on your phone. Make your choices and submit.

4

Keep your receipt

You get a random receipt code. After the vote closes you can find your ballot with it.

Why you can trust it

Trust, anonymity and checkability matter more here than features. These promises are built into the database and covered by automated tests, not left to good behavior.

No one can tell who voted for whom

A ballot stores only your choices and a random receipt code: no name, no slip, no time and no order. The site never logs slip codes or ballot contents.

One member, one vote

A slip works once and only for its own vote. Wrong guesses are rate limited and need a small puzzle solved in the browser, so bots cannot try codes in bulk.

Anyone can check the count

When a vote closes the site publishes every anonymous ballot and the audit numbers: slips used must equal ballots counted.

Our data stays ours

There is no voting company in the middle. We run the code ourselves on a small server, with no analytics, ads or trackers on the voting sites.

Check it yourself

Every vote has a public audit page. You do not have to take anyone's word for the result.

On the audit page you can

  • look up your receipt code (the search runs in your browser, so the code is never sent to us)
  • see the audit numbers: slips issued, slips used and ballots counted
  • download every anonymous ballot as JSON or CSV
  • press Recount and have your own browser add them up
  • see exactly which version of the code the site is running

For the record

  • A closed vote offers a printable PDF record with the results and audit numbers, for the board chair and two witnesses to sign.
  • The record carries a fingerprint of the published ballots, and the audit page shows the same one.
  • A tie is never broken for you: the site says Tie: revote needed and the tied choices go to a fresh vote.
  • The checklist for anyone who wants to audit the anonymity claims is in the security notes.

Paper stays in the room

The website is a trial that runs next to paper ballots, not a replacement for them.

  • During the pilot, paper is the official result. The site runs alongside it and the two counts can be compared.
  • If the internet or the site fails mid-vote, the chair switches to paper on the spot. Ballots already cast on the site stay counted, and each member who has not voted trades their unused slip for a paper ballot.
  • The chair gets a printable paper kit for exactly this. The site does not collect paper results.

Why not a form, a poll or a service?

These are good tools for what they are built for. None is built for a secret ballot.

OptionThe problem for a secret ballot
Online forms (Google, Microsoft)One answer per person needs a sign-in, so an account is tied to an answer. Responses carry a time and sit in the owner's account, and a voter cannot check theirs was counted.
Paid voting servicesA company holds the roster and the ballots, the code is closed, and each election costs money. You are trusting their word.
Chat polls and poll sites"Anonymous" is a setting the owner controls, and times or addresses can still be recorded. Without a login, nothing stops one person voting twice.
Raising handsEveryone sees how everyone votes, which is what a secret ballot exists to prevent.

What it cannot do

We would rather you hear this from us.

  • On a small board, a unanimous result tells everyone how each member voted. Paper has the same limit.
  • Anything that shows results early can hint at how the last voter voted, so running totals are off unless the chair turns them on.
  • The person who administers the database could read what is stored, though nothing stored says who cast which ballot.
  • Your own phone and the room around you are outside what a website can protect.

About

Who built it

chisBallot was built by Glen Souza, a Citrus Hills parent, for the school council board. It is free and open source: you do not have to trust the builder, because the code and every count can be checked.

Under the hood

.NET with a Blazor WebAssembly voting site, a separate sign-in-only admin site and PostgreSQL. It runs on one small server behind a Cloudflare tunnel, and every change is tested and deployed in the open on GitHub.
